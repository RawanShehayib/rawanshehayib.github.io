About

Background

I'm a junior at the University of Notre Dame studying Computer Engineering with a minor in Energy Studies, graduating May 2028 as a full scholarship recipient. Most of my work lives at the intersection of hardware and software: navigating sensors, building control systems, and training models that have to satisfy both the math and the physical constraints around them.

On campus, I co-lead IrishSat's computing team, where we build software management systems and develop models that satisfy both control and hardware needs. For two academic years, I've worked on Python-based systems that navigate sensors, reaction wheels, computer vision algorithms, and orbital simulations.

Alongside team projects, I'm drawn to research. I've worked on an AI-powered food information system for public nutrition, spent a summer studying bioacoustic data at Trinity College Dublin, and collaborated internationally on a USV control system for an underwater robotics project in Kyutech, Japan.

IrishSat - Lead Computing & Controls Engineer

CHARMS: attitude control without a GPS

I oversee the computing and controls function of IrishSat's student-built satellite, setting technical direction and aligning 15+ cross-functional engineers around a shared roadmap. CHARMS, our magnetorquer-only Attitude Determination and Control System, launched aboard SpaceX's Transporter-16 mission in March 2026 (IrishSat's first flight software) and I coordinated directly with NearSpace and SpaceX to make sure every software requirement was met before launch. On the technical side, I built a Python-based sensor management system for our Raspberry Pi satellite simulator that cut debugging time by 40%, and designed the computer vision algorithms behind autonomous horizon detection, validated against 80+ images so that the satellite can determine its orientation without ground-station intervention.

Trinity College Dublin - Research Assistant, ML for Audio Signal Processing

Fine-tuning audio models on bioacoustic data

Working with Professor Nils Peters, I'm developing a fine-tuning and multimodal retrieval framework to improve detection reliability and enable archive-wide pattern search for detecting environmental events across years of recordings. I standardized 5,445 human annotations across inconsistently formatted files into a unified 16-class taxonomy, and I'll be presenting findings at an upcoming poster session with a live user-interface model, translating the technical results for a non-technical audience.

DSP Audio ML Fine-tuning Acoustic Ecology
Kyutech University - Underwater Robotics Intern

iOS teleoperation for an unmanned surface vehicle

Built a native iOS control app in SwiftUI that connects to ROS2 over WebSocket, enabling real-time teleoperation with a live video feed and connection signal monitoring. I resolved network configuration failures across all three environments - simulation, testbed, and live hardware - taking the pipeline from simulation to physical control, in collaboration with a four-member international team spanning Japan and the U.S.
